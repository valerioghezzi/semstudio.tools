Intensive Longitudinal Reliability

A measurement taken many times on the same person carries more than one kind of error, and more than one kind of reliability follows. This page reads the output of a two-level dynamic structural equation model and reports the decomposition of the variance of the measure, the reliability for differences between persons and the reliability for the fluctuations within a person, together with the amount by which measurement error attenuates the autoregressive effect. It also computes the family of coefficients defined for a design with moments nested in days, in which reliability depends on how far apart in time the two measurements are.

What to request from Mplus. Nothing beyond the standard output of the dynamic model: the page reads the parameters of the between level and of the within level from MODEL RESULTS. Separating measurement error from the innovations needs a model that contains it, either several indicators per occasion or a single indicator with the measurement error made a parameter of its own; a model without one leaves the two confounded, which is the point Schuurman and Hamaker (2019) make. A warning printed by Mplus is reported and does not remove a result the file supports; what removes a result is a quantity that is missing or inadmissible.

Reliability at each distance in time

With moments nested in days, the agreement between two measurements depends on whether they belong to the same day and on how many moments or days separate them. Lin, Tuerlinckx and Vanbelle (2026) define a family of coefficients for that design from the variance components of a three-level model with an autoregressive process at each time scale. The components are typed in here, because the model that yields them is not one Mplus fits in this form.

Methods and formulas

The variance of a repeated measurement

In a two-level autoregressive model with measurement error the observed score of person i at occasion t is the person's mean, plus a within-person fluctuation that follows an autoregressive process, plus measurement error. Taken over all persons and occasions, its variance divides into three parts (Schuurman & Hamaker, 2019, Eq. 5):

V⁡(y)=ψμ2+E⁡[τy2]+γσ2

Here ψ²μ is the variance of the person means, that is the between-person or trait variance; E[τ²y] is the expectation over persons of the variance of the autoregressive process, that is the systematic within-person fluctuation; and γσ² is the expectation of the measurement error variance. For a first-order process the variance of the fluctuation of person i is the stationary variance of an autoregression, τy2=ζi2/(1−φi2), with ζ² the innovation variance and φ the autoregressive effect; the multivariate form is Equation 6 of the same paper.

Two reliabilities, and what each is for

For measuring differences between persons the true score is the person's mean, and everything that fluctuates within the person is noise (Schuurman & Hamaker, 2019, Eq. 7):

relb(y)=ψμ2V⁡(y)

For measuring the fluctuations of one person the true score is that person's position in the process at that moment, and only measurement error is noise (Eq. 9):

relw(yi)=τyi2τyi2+σεi2

The second one is person-specific: two people with the same measure can have different within-person reliabilities, because their processes differ in how much systematic fluctuation they carry. The page computes it at the average parameters, which is the reliability of an average member of the group; the reliability of a particular person needs that person's own estimates, which Mplus writes with SAVEDATA when the model has random innovation variances.

What measurement error does to the dynamics

Disregarding measurement error does not only inflate the noise: it biases the autoregressive effect toward zero by exactly the factor the within-person reliability gives (Schuurman & Hamaker, 2019, Eq. 10):

φ^i=relw(yi)φi

Here φ̂i is the autoregressive effect a model without measurement error returns and φi the one of the process itself. A within-person reliability of .70 therefore turns a true carry-over of .50 into an estimate near .35, and the inertia of a person is understated by the same factor. The page reports both directions: what the estimate in the file implies about the process, and what a process with the reported parameters would look like if the error were ignored.

Two time scales

When measurements are taken at several moments within a day and over several days, the agreement between two of them depends on how far apart they are and on whether they fall on the same day. Lin, Tuerlinckx and Vanbelle (2026) define the coefficients of that design from the components of a three-level model with an autoregressive process at each scale. Writing σ²s for the variance between persons, σ²d for the variance between days within a person, τ²d and τ²m for the stationary variances of the day-level and moment-level processes, σ²ε for the measurement error and φd and φm for the two autoregressive effects, the between-person coefficients are

ρBM(Δ)=σs2+σd2+τd2+τm2φmΔσs2+σd2+τd2+τm2+σε2,ρBD(Δ)=σs2+τd2φdΔσs2+σd2+τd2+τm2+σε2

The first is the agreement between two moments of the same day separated by Δ moments, the second between two days separated by Δ days. The limit of the first as Δ goes to zero is what the authors call instantaneity: the agreement between a measurement and a hypothetical parallel one taken at the same instant, which differs from it only by the realized measurement error.

The within-person coefficients drop the between-person variance from both parts of the ratio, since they describe one person:

ρMW(Δ)=σd2+τd2+τm2φmΔσd2+τd2+τm2+σε2,ρDW(Δ)=τd2φdΔσd2+τd2+τm2+σε2

The first is the agreement between two moments of one person separated by Δ moments, the second between two days of one person separated by Δ days.

Two further coefficients are reported. The first is the agreement between the averages of the M moments of two days, where averaging divides the moment-level process variance and the measurement error by the number of moments averaged:

ρ―DW(Δ)=τd2φdΔσd2+τd2+τm2/M+σε2/M

Here M is the number of moments averaged within a day, which is the value set above the table.

The second is the limit of the between-person coefficient as the distance grows without bound, which the autoregressive terms take to zero:

limΔ→∞ρBM(Δ)=σs2σs2+σd2+τd2+τm2+σε2

What is left is the share of the variance that belongs to the stable differences between persons: what the measure retains of a person over the long run.

Self-test

Adding ?selftest=1 to the address of this page runs its numerical checks in the browser and prints the outcome at the top. They cover the reading of a two-level dynamic output, the stationary variance of the autoregressive process, the decomposition of the variance and the two reliabilities against their definitions, the attenuation factor, and the whole family of coefficients at both time scales, including the limits at a lag of zero and at a long lag.
