Reliability coefficients

This page reads the output file of a confirmatory factor analysis run in Mplus and computes, for every factor in it, the reliability of the sum of its items. With continuous indicators it reports omega, average variance extracted and Cronbach's alpha; with ordered categorical indicators it reports the Green and Yang (2009) omega for the observed sum, omega on the underlying response variables y*, ordinal alpha and average variance extracted on y*. Every number it uses is read from the file, and none of them can be typed in.

What to request from Mplus. OUTPUT: SAMPSTAT STDYX; — STDYX supplies the standardized loadings, which are the loadings the ordinal coefficients are computed from, and SAMPSTAT supplies the matrix that alpha is computed from: the estimated covariance matrix with continuous items, the polychoric correlation matrix with items declared CATEGORICAL. Whatever is missing is named in the results.

What to do

  1. Open the Mplus input file of your confirmatory factor analysis, the file with the extension .inp, and add this line to it, exactly as it is written here: OUTPUT: SAMPSTAT STDYX;
  2. Run that input in Mplus. The program writes a plain text file with the same name as the input and the extension .out, in the same folder as the input.
  3. Drag the .out file onto the box below, or press Choose the .out file and pick it there. The file is read inside your browser and nothing leaves your computer.
  4. Wait for the list headed What the file contained. It names the estimator, the factors, the items of each factor and the sections the coefficients need, one line each.
  5. Read the coefficients in the table printed for each factor, and the summary table under them. The page asks you for nothing: the loadings, the residual variances, the factor variances, the thresholds and the sample matrix all come from the file, and no field on this page can change them.
  6. Copy the MODEL CONSTRAINT syntax printed under the tables if you also want a standard error and a confidence interval for omega, and run it in Mplus.

The page refuses a file that is not an Mplus output, a file with no MODEL RESULTS section, and a file whose SUMMARY OF ANALYSIS gives no estimator, because which estimator was used decides which coefficients are defined. It also refuses one factor at a time: a factor whose scale it cannot read, or whose solution is inadmissible, is named and left without a coefficient rather than given one computed from a value nobody supplied. In each case it says what is missing and which OUTPUT option puts it in the file.

Methods and formulas

Omega with continuous indicators

Omega (McDonald, 1999) is the reliability of the sum of the items implied by the estimated one-factor measurement model. It is computed from the unstandardized parameters printed under MODEL RESULTS:

ω = (Σi λi)2 ψ(Σi λi)2 ψ + Σi θii + 2 Σi < j θij

Here λi is the unstandardized loading of item i on the factor, ψ is the variance of the factor, θii is the residual variance of item i, and θij is the residual covariance between items i and j, summed over the pairs with i < j and counted twice because it enters the variance of the sum twice.

ψ is read from the output: it is 1.000 when the factor is identified by fixing its variance, and it is the value printed under Variances when the marker-variable method is used. For an endogenous factor Mplus prints the residual variance instead, and the total variance is recovered as ψ = θ / (1 − R2) from the R-SQUARE section; when R-SQUARE is absent the page says so rather than substituting a value. A residual covariance between items of the same factor belongs to the total variance of that sum; one between items of different factors belongs to neither sum and enters no coefficient.

Average variance extracted

AVE = Σi λi2 ψΣi λi2 ψ + Σi θii

The symbols are those of the previous formula: λi the unstandardized loading of item i, ψ the variance of the factor, θii the residual variance of item i.

AVE (Fornell & Larcker, 1981) is the mean proportion of item variance accounted for by the factor. It refers to the individual items rather than to the sum, so it is almost always lower than omega.

Cronbach's alpha

Alpha (Cronbach, 1951) is computed from the estimated covariance matrix that SAMPSTAT prints, restricted to the items of the factor:

α = kk − 1 × (1 − Σi σiiσ2sum)

Here k is the number of items of the factor, σii is the variance of item i from that matrix, and σ2sum is the variance of the total, that is the sum of every element of the submatrix, diagonal and off-diagonal alike.

Alpha is the value reliability takes when the items are tau-equivalent, that is when their unstandardized loadings are equal. When tau-equivalence does not hold, alpha is a lower bound and omega is the coefficient that matches the estimated model (Cho, 2016; Flora, 2020). The page prints the ratio between the largest and the smallest loading in absolute value as a descriptive indication; the formal comparison is a chi-square difference test between the congeneric and the tau-equivalent model.

Ordered categorical indicators

With CATEGORICAL ARE and a weighted least squares estimator, the loadings refer to the underlying response variables y*, not to the observed responses. A star marks a quantity on that metric. Omega on it uses the standardized loadings:

ωy* = (Σi λ*i)2(Σi λ*i)2 + Σi θ*ii + 2 Σi < j θ*ij

Here λ*i is the standardized loading of item i on the y* metric, θ*ii is its residual variance on that metric, and θ*ij is the residual correlation between items i and j, summed over the pairs with i < j.

When Mplus does not print residual variances in the standardized solution, which is the usual case under the delta parameterization, θ*ii = 1 − λ*i2 is used. This coefficient describes the sum of the y*, a score nobody computes.

The average variance extracted on the same metric follows from that identity, which makes the denominator equal to the number of items:

AVEy* = Σi λ*i2Σi λ*i2 + Σi θ*ii = 1k Σi λ*i2

The symbols are those of the previous formula, with k the number of items of the factor.

Ordinal alpha (Zumbo, Gadermann & Zeisser, 2007) is alpha applied to the polychoric correlation matrix of the items, taken from SAMPSTAT:

αordinal = kk − 1 × (1 − kΣi Σj ρij)

Here k is the number of items and ρij is the polychoric correlation between items i and j, the double sum running over every element of the matrix, so that the diagonal contributes k.

It is computed only when that matrix is positive definite: the polychoric correlations are estimated one pair at a time, so the assembled matrix need not be one that any set of variables could have produced, and alpha on an indefinite matrix is an arithmetic value rather than the reliability of a score. It is also a coefficient on the y* metric and it is generally higher than the reliability of the observed sum.

Green and Yang omega

The Green and Yang (2009) coefficient is the reliability of the sum of the observed ordinal responses. It reconstructs the variances and covariances of the observed items from the thresholds and from the correlations between the underlying variables, using the bivariate normal distribution function:

Cov(Xi, Xj) = Σc Σd [ Φ2(τic, τjd; ρij) − Φ(τic) Φ(τjd) ]

Here Xi is the observed response to item i, c and d run over the thresholds of items i and j, τic is the c-th threshold of item i, ρij is the correlation between the two underlying variables, Φ2 is the bivariate standard normal distribution function and Φ the univariate one.

The correlations ρij are the ones implied by the measurement model, not the entries of the polychoric matrix printed by Mplus: for the numerator, which is the variance of the sum attributable to the common factor, ρij = λ*i λ*j including the diagonal, where ρii = λ*i2; for the denominator, which is the total variance of the observed sum, ρij is that product plus any residual correlation the model estimates between items i and j, and ρii = 1, which returns the variance of the observed item.

ωGY = Σi Σj Covcommon(Xi, Xj)Σi Σj Covtotal(Xi, Xj)

Here Covcommon is the covariance rebuilt with the correlations implied by the common factor alone and Covtotal the one rebuilt with the correlations the whole model implies, each double sum running over every pair of items of the factor and over the diagonal.

The diagonal of the denominator is the variance of the observed item, obtained from its thresholds alone. For an item scored 1 to C with thresholds τ1 to τC−1, write qc = 1 − Φ(τc) for the probability of a response above the c-th threshold. Then

E(X) = 1 + Σc qc

Here E(X) is the mean of the observed response, c runs over the C − 1 thresholds of the item and qc is the probability just defined.

Var(X) = Σc (2c − 1) qc − (Σc qc)2

Here Var(X) is the variance of the observed response and c and qc are as above.

This is the same quantity the double sum over thresholds returns with ρ = 1, and is used because it needs no quadrature.

Φ2 is evaluated from the one-dimensional integral representation of the bivariate normal distribution function, with a 24-node Gauss–Legendre quadrature. The integral is first mapped by the substitution t = sin θ: in the direct form the factor 1 / √(1 − t2) has an integrable singularity at t = ±1 that polynomial quadrature approximates poorly, and the case of two equal arguments, which is exactly the diagonal of the numerator, is the one where the error is largest. After the substitution the integrand is smooth, and for |ρ| close to 1 the interval is split into subintervals that are packed geometrically toward the endpoint.

The formula assumes that the responses arise from normally distributed underlying variables, so it applies to a probit link. Mplus reports the link on the Link line of SUMMARY OF ANALYSIS. Weighted least squares estimators use a probit link; maximum likelihood with categorical items uses a logit link unless LINK = PROBIT is requested, and in that case the thresholds are on the logistic metric and this page does not compute the coefficient.

Confidence intervals

The coefficients on this page are point estimates computed from the parameter estimates. A standard error and a confidence interval for omega are obtained by defining it as a model parameter with MODEL CONSTRAINT and requesting CINTERVAL (Raykov, 1997). The page writes that syntax for the factors and items of the file you loaded. Point estimates of λ and θ are the same under ML and MLR; what changes with MLR is the standard errors, and therefore the intervals.

What these numbers do not settle

Values near .70 are often quoted as a minimum for research on group means, and .80 or higher for decisions about individuals; these are conventions of the literature and are used here only as rough guidance, not as a threshold a scale passes or fails (Cho, 2016; Flora, 2020). A reliability coefficient does not by itself establish that a scale is adequate: it is conditional on the model that produced it, on the sample it was estimated in, and on the use the score is put to, and it says nothing about the validity of the interpretation. A coefficient computed from an inadmissible solution is not a reliability at all, and the page marks those cases instead of reporting the number.

Two coefficients cannot be recovered from an Mplus output with categorical items: the classical alpha on Pearson correlations between the observed responses, and the omega variants that use the observed variance of the total score. Both need the raw data.
