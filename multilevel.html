Multilevel Reliability

With nested data a scale has more than one reliability, and which one is wanted depends on what the construct is taken to be and on which score is used. This page reads the output of a two-level factor model and reports the intraclass correlation of each item, the design effect, the reliability at each level and the coefficients Lai (2021) derives for a within-cluster construct, for a shared cluster-level construct and for a configural construct. It also assembles the level-specific fit of the model by the partially saturated model method of Ryu and West (2009), and writes the four inputs that method needs.

What to request from Mplus. OUTPUT: STDYX; on a TYPE = TWOLEVEL analysis. The unstandardized parameters are what the coefficients are computed from, as in the syntax Lai (2021) publishes; the standardized ones are what the construct replicability H of Hancock and Mueller (2001), which Geldhof, Preacher and Zyphur (2014) call maximal reliability needs. The number of clusters and the size of each are printed by Mplus on its own, and the harmonic mean of the cluster sizes, which two of the coefficients need, is computed from them. A warning printed by Mplus is reported and does not remove a result the file supports; what removes a result is a quantity that is missing or inadmissible.

What to do

  1. Fit the two-level factor model in Mplus with STDYX in the OUTPUT command and keep the .out file.
  2. Drop it on the box below.
  3. Say what the construct is taken to be. The choice is not a detail of estimation: a construct that exists within clusters, one that is shared by the members of a cluster and one that exists at both levels are three different things, and each has its own coefficient.
  4. Read the coefficients with the note beside each one, which says which score it is the reliability of.

Level-specific fit

A two-level model is tested by one chi-square that mixes the two levels, and the within-level part, resting on many more observations, dominates it: a between-level model that fits badly can pass unnoticed. Ryu and West (2009) separate the two by fitting the model with one level saturated at a time. This page writes the four inputs that method needs and assembles the table from the four outputs.

  1. Press Write the four inputs. The page writes them from the model of the file already loaded, or from the description you type below when no file is loaded.
  2. Run all four in Mplus.
  3. Drop the four .out files on the box below, in any order. The page recognizes each of them by its model.
One factor to a line, written as in Mplus. Used only when no file has been loaded above; with a file, the model in the file is used.

Methods and formulas

What is nested in what

The variance of an item observed on individuals nested in clusters splits into a part that varies between clusters and a part that varies within them. The intraclass correlation is the share of the first,

ICC=σb2σb2+σw2,Deff=1+(n―−1)ICC

Here σ²b and σ²w are the between-cluster and within-cluster variances of the item, n̄ the average cluster size and Deff the design effect, the factor by which the clustering inflates the sampling variance of a mean compared with a simple random sample of the same size. Mplus prints the intraclass correlations of a two-level analysis; the page reads them when they are there and computes them from the variance components when they are not.

Reliability at each level

Geldhof, Preacher and Zyphur (2014) apply the ordinary coefficients to the parameters of each level of the model:

ωw=(∑iλiw)2ψw(∑iλiw)2ψw+∑iθiw

Here λwi is the within-level loading of item i, ψw the within-level factor variance, which the model usually fixes at one, and θwi its within-level residual variance. The between-level coefficient has the same form with the between-level parameters. Their maximal reliability uses the standardized loadings of the level in question, H=[1+1/∑iλi21−λi2]−1. The same authors report that the between-level maximal reliability is badly biased upward when the between-level reliability and the intraclass correlations are low and the clusters are small, and write that they cannot recommend its use in applied multilevel research; the page prints it with that warning attached.

Which coefficient goes with which construct

Lai (2021) shows that the coefficients above are the reliability of no score anyone computes, and derives the reliability of the scores that are actually used, one for each meaning the construct can have. The formulas below are the ones his supplementary syntax computes.

For a within-cluster construct the score of interest is the individual's, and its reliability is ωw above, estimated in a model whose between level is left saturated so that the within-level structure is not distorted by a between-level model that may be wrong.

For a configural construct the raw score of an individual carries both levels, and its reliability is

ω2L=(∑iλi)2(1+ψb)(∑iλi)2(1+ψb)+∑iθib+∑iθiw

Here λi is the loading of item i, held equal across the two levels as a configural construct requires, ψb is the between-level factor variance with the within-level one fixed at one, and θbi and θwi are the residual variances at the two levels.

The reliability of the cluster mean of that same score carries the sampling error of a mean over a finite number of members:

ωb=(∑iλi)2ψb(∑iλi)2ψb+∑iθib+∑iθiw+(∑iλi)2n―

Here n̄ is the harmonic mean of the cluster sizes, which is the value Lai uses in his syntax and the one this page computes from the cluster sizes Mplus prints. The term divided by it is the variance of the cluster mean of the within-level part of the composite: with large clusters it vanishes and the coefficient approaches the one computed from the between-level parameters alone, and with small clusters it dominates.

For a shared construct there is no within-level factor, since what is measured belongs to the cluster and the differences among its members are disagreement. The within level is then left saturated and its whole composite variance enters as error:

ωb=(∑iλib)2ψb(∑iλib)2ψb+∑iθib+1′Σw1n―

Here Σw is the within-level covariance matrix of the items, left unstructured, and 1′Σw1 the sum of all its elements, that is the within-level variance of the unit-weighted total. The page computes it from the within-level variances and covariances the file prints, and says so when the within level of the model is not saturated, since the quantity is then the one the structure implies rather than the one the data show.

Level-specific fit

Ryu and West (2009) obtain the fit of one level by saturating the other. Fitting the model with the within level saturated gives a chi-square that reflects the between-level model alone, and the reverse gives one that reflects the within-level model. Their fit indices use those statistics with a baseline that is itself partially saturated, so that the saturated half of the model does not inflate the index:

CFIPS−B=1−max(χPS−B2−dfPS−B,0)max(χI−B2−dfI−B,0)

Here χ²PS−B and dfPS−B are those of the model with the between level hypothesized and the within level saturated, and χ²I−B and dfI−B those of the model with the between level an independence model and the within level saturated. The within-level index is the mirror image of this one.

RMSEAPS−B=χPS−B2−dfPS−BdfPS−BJ,RMSEAPS−W=χPS−W2−dfPS−WdfPS−WN

Here J is the number of clusters and N the total number of observations: the between-level index is scaled by the number of clusters, which is the sample size that level rests on, and the within-level one by the number of observations. Both are set to zero when the quantity under the root is negative.

Self-test

Adding ?selftest=1 to the address of this page runs its numerical checks in the browser and prints the outcome at the top. They cover the reading of a two-level output, of the cluster sizes and of the intraclass correlations, the harmonic mean, every coefficient against the values the published syntax of Lai (2021) returns for the same parameters, the level-specific fit indices against their definitions, and the cases the page has to refuse.
