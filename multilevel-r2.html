Multilevel Variance Explained

How much of the variance a two-level model accounts for has no single answer: the outcome varies within clusters and between them, and a predictor can act through a fixed slope, through the variation of a slope, or through the variation of the intercept. This page reads one fitted model and reports the whole partition, together with the twelve proportions Rights and Sterba (2019) define on it. Nothing is typed in and no second model has to be fitted.

What to request from Mplus. TYPE = TWOLEVEL RANDOM; with OUTPUT: SAMPSTAT;. The parameters of the model are read from MODEL RESULTS; the variances and covariances of the predictors, which the model does not estimate when they are exogenous, are read from the sample statistics of the two levels, or from MODEL RESULTS when the model does estimate them. The page says for every predictor where its variance came from. The measures are defined for a model whose level-1 predictors are centered on their cluster means, which is what DEFINE: CENTER x (GROUPMEAN); does.

What to do

  1. Fit the model with SAMPSTAT in the OUTPUT command.
  2. Drop the .out file on the box below.
  3. Read what the page found before reading the measures: the sources of the variance are only as good as the quantities they were built from, and the page names every one of them.

Methods and formulas

Why one model is enough

The older measures of explained variance in multilevel models are built by fitting two models, an empty one and the model of interest, and comparing their variance components. LaHuis, Hartman, Hakoyama and Clark (2014) put those measures through a simulation: because they subtract one estimate from another, they can come out negative when nothing is wrong, and the measure for the second level in particular was biased and inefficient enough that they do not recommend it. The measure built instead on the full partition of the variance of a single fitted model was among the least biased when the model carried random slopes, and cannot be negative; its disadvantage, in their words, was that it is tedious to calculate. This page performs that calculation.

The partition

The predictors of the first level are the ones that vary within a cluster and the predictors of the second level the ones that vary between clusters, which is the within level and the between level of the model as Mplus prints it. Writing a two-level model with level-1 predictors xw centered on their cluster means, level-2 predictors xb, and the subset w of the level-1 predictors whose slopes vary between clusters, the variance the model implies for the outcome is the sum of five terms (Rights & Sterba, 2019, Eq. 10):

var(yij)=γw′Φwγw+γb′Φbγb+tr(TΣ)+τ00+σ2

Here γw and γb are the fixed effects of the level-1 and level-2 predictors, Φw and Φb the covariance matrices of those predictors, T the covariance matrix of the random slopes, Σ the covariance matrix of the predictors those slopes belong to, τ00 the variance of the random intercept and σ2 the level-1 residual variance. The five terms are, in order (Eq. 11 to 15), the variance attributable to the level-1 predictors through their fixed slopes, to the level-2 predictors through their fixed slopes, to the level-1 predictors through the variation and covariation of their slopes, to the cluster-specific means through the variation of the intercept, and to the level-1 residuals.

Three of the five belong entirely to the variation within clusters, so the within-cluster variance the model implies is their sum (Eq. 16), and the remaining two make up the between-cluster variance (Eq. 17):

σw2=γw′Φwγw+tr(TΣ)+σ2,σb2=γb′Φbγb+τ00

The twelve measures

Each measure is a source of explained variance over a choice of outcome variance (Rights & Sterba, 2019, Table 1). The subscript names the denominator, t for the total variance, w for the within-cluster variance, b for the between-cluster variance; the superscript names the sources in the numerator, f1 for the level-1 predictors through fixed slopes, f2 for the level-2 predictors through fixed slopes, v for the variation of the slopes, m for the variation of the intercept, and the combinations f = f1 + f2, fv, and fvm. Seven have the total variance in the denominator, three the within-cluster variance and two the between-cluster variance. Because the level-1 residual is never explained variance at either level, the two between-cluster measures are the ones for f2 and for m separately; their sum is one, which is why no combined between-cluster measure is defined.

The seven total measures are not alternatives to one another and are not meant to be reported one at a time. Rights and Sterba recommend giving the four that each carry a single source, Rt2(f1), Rt2(f2), Rt2(v) and Rt2(m), together with their level-specific counterparts, and reading them against the picture of the whole partition rather than in isolation. The bar chart above is that picture, in the form of their Figure 1.

Where each quantity comes from

The fixed effects, the random slope covariances, the intercept variance and the residual variance are parameters of the model and are read from MODEL RESULTS: the level-1 fixed slope of a predictor whose slope is random is the mean of that slope at the between level, which is where Mplus puts it when the slope is defined with s | y ON x;. The covariance matrices of the predictors are not parameters of the model when the predictors are exogenous and observed, which is the usual case: the model conditions on them. They are read instead from the estimated sample statistics of the two levels, which SAMPSTAT prints, and from MODEL RESULTS when the model does estimate them. The table above names the source of every one of them, and when one is missing the page says which predictor it belongs to and computes nothing, rather than filling the gap.

Two conditions are checked and reported rather than assumed. The measures are defined for level-1 predictors centered on their cluster means, so the page looks for a CENTER statement with GROUPMEAN in the echoed input and says whether it found one. And the sum of the five terms is compared with the variance of the outcome in the sample statistics: a large discrepancy means that some source of variance in the model was not read, and the page reports the discrepancy so that the measures are not taken for a complete partition when they are not one.

Self-test

Adding ?selftest=1 to the address of this page runs its numerical checks in the browser and prints the outcome at the top. They cover the reading of a two-level output written for the purpose, the quadratic forms and the trace against their definitions written out a second way, the requirement that the five proportions of the total variance sum to one and that the level-specific ones sum to one within their own level, the equality of each combined measure with the sum of its parts, the behavior of the measures in the cases where a source is absent, and the outputs the page has to refuse.
